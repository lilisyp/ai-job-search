# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Company X — Nanofiltration Pass (Disciplined IC / Technical & Commercial DD)
**S:** BASF Venture Capital evaluated a solvent-resistant nanofiltration membrane startup claiming 30–50% process-energy savings; management projections were optimistic and the space was crowded.
**T:** As deal lead, determine whether the technical and commercial thesis justified investment at the proposed valuation.
**A:** Isolated chemical-solution compatibility and nano-particle separation as the one technical variable that mattered; interviewed pilot and prospective customers on pricing, willingness-to-pay, and acquisition timelines rather than relying on management; built the financial projection from scratch and stress-tested scenarios.
**R:** Presented an evidence-led recommendation against proceeding at IC, citing valuation and management concerns — deal killed on facts, not momentum.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a deal you passed on", "How do you handle disagreement with founders or sponsors?", "Describe your due diligence approach"

### 2. EAVision — Agro-Drone Investment (Portfolio Win / Multi-Round)
**S:** BASF VC sought Asia deep-tech exposure in intelligent hardware with credible path to scale in agriculture.
**T:** Source, evaluate, and lead investment in a promising agro-drone company through multiple rounds.
**A:** Led diligence and internal pitching; invested millions USD across 2020 and 2022 rounds; remained engaged as board observer through commercialization milestones.
**R:** Portfolio company progressing toward IPO filing; demonstrates sustained conviction and portfolio value creation beyond initial check.
**Use for:** "Walk me through an investment you're proud of", "Board observer experience", "How do you support portfolio companies"

### 3. Prismlab — SABIC First Direct Equity (First-in-Market / Returns)
**S:** SABIC Ventures needed to establish direct equity investing credibility in China's 3D-printing sector.
**T:** Lead the firm's first direct equity investment in a Chinese 3D-printing startup.
**A:** Ran full evaluation and closing process for Prismlab (2018); structured and executed SABIC's first direct China equity deal after the 2017 fund investment milestone.
**R:** Valuation more than tripled by 2022; company now filing for IPO — validates early direct-investment platform build.
**Use for:** "Building a new investment platform", "First-time market entry", "Measuring investment success"

### 4. Voith China M&A — Zero to First Deal (Corp Dev / Build Function)
**S:** Voith had no established corporate development or M&A function in China despite strategic growth ambitions.
**T:** Stand up corp dev/M&A capability and deliver Voith's first China M&A transaction.
**A:** Built the function, led strategy work with BCG/Bain/McKinsey on cross-regional projects, sourced and executed the first acquisition.
**R:** Voith's first China M&A completed; established repeatable platform for inorganic growth in the market.
**Use for:** "Tell me about building something from scratch", "Corp dev leadership", "Working with consultants and HQ"

### 5. Startup–BASF BU Collaborations (Portfolio Value Creation / Strategic CVC)
**S:** Portfolio companies in alternative feedstock, hydrogen carriers, and cosmetic ingredients needed pathways to commercial validation beyond capital.
**T:** Connect startups to relevant BASF business units to accelerate pilots and strategic fit.
**A:** Identified BU stakeholders, aligned technical and commercial narratives, and brokered collaboration threads across multiple themes.
**R:** Active collaboration pipeline between portfolio and operating units — strategic CVC value beyond financial return.
**Use for:** "What does strategic VC mean to you?", "How do you create value post-investment?", "Cross-functional stakeholder management"

### 6. PwC Transaction Services — Warburg/TPG/Sequoia Mandates (FDD Foundation)
**S:** Global PE investors and multinationals required buy-side and vendor financial due diligence on manufacturing, retail, and industrial deals.
**T:** Lead QoE, working-capital, and net-debt workstreams and manage deal teams under tight timelines.
**A:** Four years in PwC TS after three years in assurance; delivered FDD for leading sponsors including Warburg Pincus, TPG, and Sequoia.
**R:** Built the analytical foundation (QoE discipline, scepticism of management numbers) that later underpins CVC investment decisions.
**Use for:** "Why transition from advisory to investing?", "Financial due diligence strengths", "Managing complex workstreams"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave BASF?"
> My role ended on 30 June 2026 when BASF Venture Capital closed its Hong Kong office (along with Brazil and Los Angeles) as part of a global restructuring. This was an organizational decision, not performance-related. I'm now focused on senior investment or strategy roles in the Greater Bay Area where I can continue bridging deep tech, strategic capital, and cross-border stakeholders.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]."
> Acknowledge the gap honestly. Bridge to adjacent experience — e.g. operator-side fundraising draws on board observer work, IC materials, and investor relationships built over 8 years in CVC plus 4 years in transaction services. Show willingness to go deep where the role requires it.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Leading or building investment capability for a strategic/industrial platform in the GBA — whether as a senior investor owning deal flow and portfolio value creation, or as a strategy/fundraising leader inside a deep-tech scale-up. The common thread is the bridge role between technology, capital, and cross-border markets.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> Low tolerance for environments where politics override merit — mitigated by choosing platforms with genuine strategic capital and merit-based IC cultures; channel energy into rigorous analysis and transparent risk presentation rather than advocacy without evidence.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific sector fit (materials, decarbonization, deep tech), GBA mandate, strategic vs. pure-financial capital, portfolio/BU collaboration model, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "How is the investment committee structured, and what gets a fast no vs. a deep dive?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the investment team, and how do you divide sourcing vs. portfolio work?"
- "What does the path from term sheet to close typically look like here?"
- "How do you work with business units or LPs on strategic alignment?"

### About Strategy & Growth
- "What's the fund's or platform's mandate in Greater China / GBA specifically?"
- "How do you balance financial return with strategic value to the parent / LPs?"
- "What sectors are you actively leaning into vs. deprioritizing?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe decision-making culture — merit-based or relationship-driven?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for Hong Kong-based work with travel in the GBA?"
- "What do people who thrive on this team have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions (deal structuring, DD, portfolio)
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
